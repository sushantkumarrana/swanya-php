Water for Injection (WFI) is the single most important raw material in a sterile-liquids plant. In products like Sterile Water for Injection it *is* the product; in saline and respules it is more than 99% of the formulation. This article walks through a typical WFI system — the one Swanya operates in Baddi follows the same architecture.

## Step 1: Purified Water

WFI starts as **Purified Water**. Feed water passes through ozone treatment, UV, a multigrade filter, a softener, reverse osmosis, electrodeionisation and a final UV stage before storage. You can explore an animated version of this train on our [manufacturing page](/manufacturing#water-systems).

## Step 2: Distillation

Purified Water is fed to a **multi-column still**. Pure steam supplies the first column; raw (plant) steam provides the heating energy. Vapour rises through successive columns, is condensed, and the condensate — now free of pyrogens and non-volatile impurities — is collected as WFI.

## Step 3: Hot storage and the loop

Freshly generated WFI is held in a storage tank **maintained at 80 °C and above**. The tank breathes through a hydrophobic **vent filter** so that air entering as levels drop is sterile. From the tank, WFI is pumped continuously around a **distribution loop** to every point of use and back — hot, turbulent circulation is the primary defence against biofilm.

## Step 4: Sampling and testing

A WFI loop is sampled routinely at generation, storage and user points. The four core tests are:

| Test | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Conductivity** | Ionic purity — confirms the still and EDI are performing |
| **pH** | Consistency of the water chemistry |
| **Pyrogen / bacterial endotoxin test** | The loop is free of Gram-negative bacterial debris |
| **Microbial limit test** | Viable counts remain within alert and action limits |

Results are trended, not just pass/failed, so that a slow drift is caught long before a limit is breached.
