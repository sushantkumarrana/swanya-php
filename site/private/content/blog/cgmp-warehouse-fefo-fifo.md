Visitors tend to focus on the filling line, but auditors spend a surprising amount of time in the **warehouse**. Every raw material, excipient and packaging component passes through it, and the controls applied there decide whether what reaches the manufacturing suite is what the batch record says it is.

## Controlled storage

A cGMP warehouse separates materials by status — **quarantine, under test, approved, rejected** — physically or through a validated system, and stores temperature-sensitive materials in controlled conditions with continuous monitoring.

## The sampling booth

Before an incoming API or excipient can be approved, Quality Control samples it. Opening a drum on an open floor would expose the material to the environment and the operator to the material. A **sampling booth** — a dedicated enclosure with laminar airflow and its own filtration — solves both problems. Swanya operates a separate sampling booth for APIs and excipients so that potent materials and bulk excipients are never sampled in the same space.

## Dispensing under RLAF

Weighing the exact quantity for a batch is the next exposure point. **Reverse Laminar Air Flow (RLAF) dispensing booths** pull air *away* from the operator and through HEPA filtration, protecting both the material and the person doing the weighing.

## FEFO versus FIFO

- **FIFO — First In, First Out** — consumes the oldest received lot first.
- **FEFO — First Expiry, First Out** — consumes the lot that expires soonest first, regardless of when it arrived.

FEFO is the stricter rule for pharmaceuticals because a later delivery can carry an earlier expiry date. Swanya's warehouse practice is FEFO/FIFO: expiry governs, and receipt order breaks ties.
